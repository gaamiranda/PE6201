# 1. Why an agent at all

Health-insurance claim first response requires evidence from policies, coverage rules, pre-authorisations and claim history. A deterministic workflow could implement our current routing rules. Within A2's prescribed rung-seven architecture, we explored a model selecting its next tool from returned evidence. This permits selective retrieval and early exit: a lapsed policy ends the investigation, while a covered procedure may require another pre-authorisation lookup. These capabilities are also programmable as workflow branches; our experiments do not establish an agent's superiority over that alternative.

The lower rungs remain useful components. A single prompt needs evidence supplied beforehand; chains and routing encode control flow; parallel calls shorten independent checks but cannot remove dependencies. Orchestrator-worker and evaluator-optimiser patterns organise or refine work, but do not themselves settle which evidence is sufficient for our claim decision. Our loop retrieves evidence until it can act, request a document or escalate. Its state-changing tool, `issue_decision_letter`, writes a decision record behind an autonomy gate; read-only retrieval alone would not address that governance requirement.

Two tests constrain this choice. First, can external records correct the model promptly? Our tools return objective policy and coverage facts, but interpreting hostile narrative text lacks the same direct feedback. Second, does reliability survive multiple steps? Scripted replay achieved 57/76 combined passes (75.0%), with five median turns: implied reliability is s = P^(1/T) = 0.9441. This diagnostic assumes equally reliable, independent steps; it is neither a measured step probability nor a causal forecast. Decision-only success was 72/76, exposing a substantial gap between correct routing and adequate supporting records.

The live comparison tempered our expectations. On GPT-4o-mini, v1 achieved 28/76 combined passes and v2 achieved 31/76, while input tokens increased 37.1%. Both had four median turns. These runs show a small observed score increase, insufficient to establish a reproducible accuracy gain. More explicit interfaces aid inspection but do not guarantee better decisions. Success therefore means traceable reasons, correct routing, controlled writes, explicit missing information and defensible total cost. We retain caps and evaluate supporting records; growing trajectories or additional consequential actions would favour more deterministic control.
